Check a Qbit fee bump for later payments right before it is broadcast, and again after a background restart (#340). A bump was checked for transactions spending its original's outputs only when it was assessed and built, so a payment made while its confirm screen was open (from another wallet on the same seed, or a recipient spending their output) could still be evicted when it went out. The broadcast now asks the explorer, with the send guard's queue held, for the original's raw bytes (which must hash to its txid) and its `/tx/:txid/outspends` immediately before dispatch, and refuses before dispatch (`not_sent`, `guard_blocked`) when any output is spent or the explorer gives no answer of one well-formed entry per output within 10 seconds; the claim stays held, so the same bytes can be retried. Before refusing, the bump itself is looked up: a retry of a bump whose earlier answer was lost finds its original gone, and a found bump is reported as the success that answer lost. A restart also forgot which transaction a bump replaces, so a retry from the recovery screen was restored as a plain send and skipped the check. `QbitSendBroadcastClaim` gains `replaces`: the original's txid on a wallet-minted fee bump claim, `null` on any other wallet send. The page keeps it with the signed bytes. `restoreBroadcastClaim` refuses a bump while a later payment spends its original, and the restored claim's broadcast checks the explorer again. A wallet claim without the field was minted before it existed, and is refused rather than restored. `runWithBroadcastClaim` reads the replaced txid from the claim and no longer takes it as a fourth argument, and `broadcastIfAllowed` hands it to its broadcast callback. A provider claim's `replaces` is dropped.
