The balance read (`getQbitSpendableBalanceV2`) now rides out the explorer's transient server errors instead of losing its split, or its total, to one of them. Its address statistics, output listing and chain tip answered with a 502, 503 or 504, and a transaction lookup answered with a 500, 502, 503 or 504, are read again 500 ms and then 1,500 ms later, the explorer's 502 with `Retry-After: 60` included, while the next attempt would begin within 3 s of the first request and always within the read's deadline (`QBIT_BALANCE_RIDE_OUT`). A reply the client already retried twice by itself gets one client call more. At most 32 lookups of one read ride out (`QBIT_BALANCE_READ_RIDE_OUT_LOOKUPS`), and a lookup holds no slot of the read limiter while it pauses. A bare 500 or a refusal of the listing is judged as before, a 429 is left to the client, and an ancestor's walk is not read again.

When a request for the address's statistics goes out after the listing did and before the statistics answer, whoever sent it (the read's own later attempt, the client's own retry, or another caller's request the read joined), the listing is thrown away unclassified, stopped should it still be in flight, and asked for again, fresh, so that a block landing between the two can never make Available count an output that block spent. A read classifies one listing, and only once its statistics have answered: the one asked for with them, or else the one asked for again, which is judged as a first listing is (the explorer's limit, a bare 500, a listing cut off by the deadline). Nothing of a read that listed again is kept for reuse. What this costs: no lookup starts, and so no fact is kept, while the statistics are still being read, so the read after an outage of the statistics starts cold; and when the statistics answer later than the listing, the lookups start that much later than in 1.0.10. Usually the statistics answer first, and nothing changes. Two reads of one address that share a request for its statistics answered after both listings begin classifying together, and so may each send the same first 256 lookups, as 1.0.10 does for two reads begun together, within one reply's time of each other. Fresh requests for the statistics, which no read can join, are not counted, so another caller's fresh read costs nothing; a request for the statistics another caller sends in that window, which the read did not join, still costs one listing more. A listing newer than the statistics is used as it is, as Available is then bounded by the older statistics. The rule orders requests by when they went out, not by when the explorer read its state, so statistics asked for before the listing but served after it are still paired with the older listing, as in 1.0.10 for every read whose statistics are answered later than its listing.

A lookup that still fails no longer cancels the others at once: the lookups in flight go on, and what they read is kept for the next read, for up to 3 s after the first group of outputs of the listing it answers from failed (`QBIT_BALANCE_READ_FAILURE_GRACE_MS`), when the rest is cancelled as at the deadline. So a read with a failed lookup now answers up to 3 s after that failure, where in 1.0.10 the first failure stopped the classification and the read answered at once. Once 8 lookups have failed, or been answered with a transaction that fails the wallet's checks (`QBIT_BALANCE_READ_FAILED_LOOKUPS`), the read starts no more, and makes no further attempt of a lookup pausing or waiting for a slot. An explorer that fails every lookup is then sent at most 263 lookups by one read, the same whether the read lists again or not, as only one listing is classified; that costs at most 327 lookup requests when it answers with its own 502 (`Retry-After` past the client's cap) and at most 885 when the client retries each reply twice itself; replies whose bodies are lost can cost up to three attempts each. The answer is honest as before: whenever an output is left unread the split is unknown, `utxoReadIncomplete` and `utxoReadFailed`, never worked out from part of the outputs. Failed walks of own change alone leave the split complete, those outputs Pending, as before.

For the balance read, two things differ from what the pending changeset `balance-read-wallet-background.md` says: server errors are no longer left to the client library alone outside the Send screen's read, and the first failed funding-transaction read no longer stops the rest and cancels those in flight at once. The reads of outputs for a send, a sweep, a send plan or a spend check, the Send screen's included, behave exactly as before.

`withQbitReadRetries` takes a fourth argument, `QbitReadRetryOptions`: the schedule (`QbitRideOutSchedule`, the Send screen's `QBIT_SEND_SCREEN_RIDE_OUT` by default), `mayRideOut`, asked once at a read's first server error and refusing the ride-out unless it says yes, and `startedAt`, the time the ride-out is counted from. `QbitClassificationReads` takes a seventh constructor argument, `keeping`, a boolean, which only the balance read's classification sets.
