Sign every entry of an approved batch of P2MR PSBTs, in any order (#152). Each entry's budget is prepared before any entry signs, so once one entry had signed, `signP2mrPsbtWithCounter` refused every later entry that needed new signatures with "P2MR signature budget changed after approval". `KeyringService.openP2mrPsbtSigningBatch(address, inputs, { reuseScope? })` now opens a batch of the PSBTs one approval covers, each input with the `approvedBudget` its approval disclosed, and returns the batch's id and the whole batch's budget: every distinct signature its entries need, counted once, with the signatures remaining before the batch and after all its entries. A `reuseScope` reopens the batch open in that scope only for the same approval: the same bytes with the same approved budgets. A review whose budgets were prepared again, after an entry spent, gets a batch of its own. `signP2mrPsbtWithCounter` takes the entry's `{ id, index }` as its fourth argument, in place of the `approvedBatchDuplicate` flag. An entry signs in its batch only with the budget it was approved with; any other, even one that matches the counter now, is refused rather than checked on its own. The budget check then accepts exactly what the batch's other entries spent since the approval, measured under the counter lock: the counter they advanced and the signatures of this entry they signed and cached, as long as the batch stays within its approved total. It still refuses a signature made outside the batch, an entry signed twice, an entry of another account or PSBT, and any other drift. A replayed entry is refused as already signed, whatever budget it carries. A batch whose entry failed partway counts what that entry spent. Restored-floor challenges are still checked per entry. Opening a batch checks every entry's approved budget against the counter, so a signature made between preparing and opening refuses the batch before its approval shows. `releaseP2mrPsbtSigningBatches(ids)` closes batches, and `hasOpenP2mrPsbtSigningBatches(ids)` tells whether they are still open. Locking the wallet closes them all, and a lock while a batch opens leaves none stored. A website request's batch (opened without a `reuseScope`) closes when its request is answered or fails, and is never evicted while its request is open: with 64 batches open, opening another evicts the oldest local batch, and while only requests' batches are open it is refused ("Too many signing requests are waiting"). A local batch closes only on a lock or when it is evicted. An entry of a batch that is no longer open, closed by a lock, its request or an eviction, is refused ("This batch approval was interrupted…") instead of being checked on its own, where the first entry would sign alone and the rest would be refused.

Two cases still fail closed, as they did before batches: an entry whose signing failed cannot be retried within its batch, since its own spend is not counted and it is marked signed once its signatures are made, before the signed PSBT is assembled; and a batch with duplicates that needs more than 16 signatures from one key can be refused depending on the order its entries sign in, since the keyring caches 16 signatures per key. Neither signs past the approved budget.
