Show a large signing's progress, keep the auto-lock from ending it, and stop it when its page closes (#171). `signPsbtV2(toSignData, { progressId })` and `signLocalBatchPsbt(toSignData, review, { progressId })` send each input's progress, as `BUS_METHODS.QBIT_SIGNING_PROGRESS` tagged with that id, to the wallet page that made the call and to no other page (`BUS_EVENTS.toUIPort`). A call that came through no page, or an id that is not 1 to 64 letters, digits, `_` or `-`, gets no progress and signs as before. When that page closes (`onUiPortDisconnected`), its signing stops before its next input, since nothing can receive the signed bytes. An auto-lock that falls due during a local signing call whose page is still open, from its first check to its last input, waits for it: it locks one minute after the last such signing ends (`AUTO_LOCK_SIGNING_HANDOFF_MS`), also when a signing that succeeded ends less than a minute before it falls due. The page's `broadcastQbitTx` that follows holds it the same way, and the minute then runs from the verdict. It never locks later than 10 minutes after it fell due (`AUTO_LOCK_SIGNING_GRACE_MS`), by the wall clock or by the wait's own timer, so moving the clock back cannot stretch it. A signing is not a screen action, so back-to-back signings cannot stretch the wait, and a screen action after the deadline does not start the time over either. Closing the signing's page during the wait, or in the minute after it, ends the wait for that page: the auto-lock then locks at once past its deadline, and at its deadline before it, unless another page is still owed its minute. A computer that slept past the wait locks on waking or as soon as the signing ends, and an auto-lock held only by a page that closes locks at once. A signing call that arrives after its page closed stops before its first input. The lock button, the computer locking and the last window closing still lock at once. A signing that a lock stops now fails with `WALLET_LOCKED_DURING_SIGNING` instead of whatever the keyring hit first, such as its liveness check or "password is not a string" from the vault write; an error that carries its own code keeps it. `signLocalBatchPsbt` on a locked wallet fails with the same code, so a batch stops at its first entry after a lock.
