'@qbit/wallet-background': major

Bound, keep and time-limit the reads behind an address's balance and outputs, so the balance of an account with thousands of outputs loads again (#36, #124). Since the explorer's output-listing limit was raised from 500 to 50,000 (#169), such an address took the per-output path: one `/tx` read per output, all at once, on every poll, which timed out in the browser's queue and failed the whole balance.

- Classification reads go through `QbitReadLimiter`: at most `QBIT_CLASSIFY_MAX_READS_IN_FLIGHT` (256) in flight to one explorer, funding transactions and unconfirmed ancestors together, first come first served, across every classification through the same client; 256 is what 1.0.9 was measured to put on one HTTP/3 connection at once. A classification reads at that rate until it has started `QBIT_CLASSIFY_BURST_LOOKUPS` (4,096) transaction lookups (outputs served from kept facts or the history, or classified from the listing, cost none), and from then on holds its own lookups to `QBIT_CLASSIFY_SUSTAINED_READS_IN_FLIGHT` (32): accounts in the range 1.0.9 is known or likely to read stay inside the burst (it was measured reading 620 outputs about 256 at once; one 3,908-output account is reported not to load in it), an account needing more lookups that 1.0.9 could still read on a fast link, if any, is slower past it, and the explorer answered bursts of 502 to about 1,000 lookups a second held for ten seconds. A transaction lookup (in the slot it holds), output listing or classification tip read whose reply body could not be read after a non-transient status is read again, up to twice, after 100 and 300 ms (`withQbitReadRetries`, `isQbitBodyReadFailure`, `QBIT_BODY_READ_RETRY_PAUSES_MS`): the client library retries no lost body, and one lost in a broken connection would fail a read of 20,000. Server errors are left to the client library, as in 1.0.9, except in `readQbitAddressUtxosRidingOut`, which the controller uses only for `getQbitUtxoSnapshot(readId, true)`, a usable read id and the boolean `true` (`'true'`, `1` and anything else are no; only the send screen's own reads ask, the one it opens with and each one it makes again while open, and its prepare with no outputs loaded and its send in one transaction pass the read id alone and read as every other read): it reads a lookup that ends on 500, 502, 503 or 504, and its listing and tip on 502, 503 or 504, again after 500 ms, 1.5 s, 4 s and 8 s (`isQbitServerError`, `QBIT_SERVER_ERROR_RETRY_PAUSES_MS`), beginning no attempt once 20 s have passed since the first (`QBIT_SERVER_ERROR_RIDE_OUT_MS`), so a burst of server errors pauses that long read instead of failing it, holding its limiter slots meanwhile (up to 256 in its first 4,096 lookups, 32 after), which other reads through the same client wait behind (a balance poll was measured at 13.9 s; a riding lookup keeps its slot about 14 s to 18.5 s when the explorer answers at once with no `Retry-After` the client follows, and up to two client calls, 20.5 s, 36.5 s or 38.3 s as measured, where the client waits on one or the replies are slow); the library hands back at once a 5xx whose `Retry-After` is past its 30 s cap. A snapshot read that does not ask and joins one that does shares its ride-out; one that asks and joined a read that does not reads again on its own, riding them out, after a failure the ride-out would have made again (`isQbitRideOutFailure`). An ownership walk's read of an ancestor is read again only after a lost body. After those attempts, the first failed funding-transaction read, or the caller's signal, stops the rest and cancels those in flight; an ancestor that cannot be read still only costs its output its own-change standing.
- `QbitFundingTxReads` keeps what a funding transaction's txid fixes about each output read (coinbase or not, value, script) per API client, for at most `QBIT_FUNDING_FACTS_MAX_OUTPOINTS` (65,536) outputs, least recently used first. A classification counts every kept output of its listing as used before it reads any afresh, so what it keeps pushes out other addresses' facts first: after a switch between two accounts that fit only one at a time, a poll reads only what the other account pushed out. It is kept and served only with the script of the address being read, served only for an output the fresh listing reports confirmed, and checked against the listed value; an unconfirmed output's transaction, the tip and every ancestor's confirmation are read afresh. Every balance read, and every read of outputs with a shared reader, of an address whose script can be derived asks for the tip together with the listing, one request even for an address with no output, where 1.0.9 asked only when a coinbase output needed it. A read of outputs that a send, sweep or plan spends serves a kept answer that the transaction is not a coinbase only for an output at least 1,000 blocks deep, as deep as a coinbase output must be to mature, and reads a younger one afresh, so one wrong answer from the explorer cannot let a send select an immature reward; the balance read, whose answer is only totals, serves it at any confirmed depth. Depth is measured from the listing's own newest block, and from the tip, asked for together with the listing, for outputs that block cannot show deep: nothing but a coinbase output's maturity waits for the tip, and a tip that cannot be read, or has not answered once nothing else is left to read, costs a fresh read of those outputs only. A poll of an address whose outputs did not change reads no transaction. The kept facts of a transaction that pays the address more than 16 times are also indexed by output number, so keeping and serving them takes time linear in their number, and pushing facts out never walks the cache: the order of use is a list through the kept transactions. A read that began before a clear, its listing, statistics or walk of the history still running, keeps nothing after it and is served nothing; the send screen's snapshot, both send builders, the recovery passes and a send plan's revision take that place when they are asked, before their own first wait (`QbitClassifyOptions.facts`). The controller forgets it when the wallet locks, when its data is reset, when a keyring is removed and when the network changes, and nothing is served or kept while the wallet is locked.
- `getQbitSpendableBalanceV2` takes `QbitBalanceReadOptions` (`fundingTxReads`, `signal`, `deadlineMs`) and has one deadline for every read it makes, `QBIT_BALANCE_READ_DEADLINE_MS` (20 s). Once the address statistics are read, an output read that fails, or has not finished by the deadline, no longer rejects: the answer is the total with the split unknown and `utxoReadIncomplete: true` (and `utxoReadFailed: true` when the read failed, or ran out of time without keeping anything a later read is served, so a poll can back off), and the next read carries on from what was kept. An output the listing reports confirmed at least 1,000 blocks deep, a coinbase maturity, needs no read: the balance read classifies it from the listing alone as spendable and mature, with the listed value and the address's script, judging depth from the listing's newest block and then from the tip, asked for once with the listing, and reading those outputs as before when the tip cannot be read or has not answered once nothing else is left to read; for such an output it no longer checks the explorer's transaction against the listing, and Available stays bounded by the statistics. With no derivable address script every output is read. With a shared reader, a balance read with more outputs still to read one by one (younger confirmed ones and unconfirmed ones) than the reader keeps facts of (`maxOutpoints`, 65,536 for the controller's) reads none of them, at most the tip: it answers at once with the total, the split unknown, `utxoReadIncomplete` and `utxoReadFailed`, so a poll backs off. A read of outputs for a send or a plan (`readQbitAddressUtxos`) lists a deep output from the listing as well, as a candidate, and every input a transaction spends is read and checked against its funding transaction before it is signed (`checkQbitSpendInputs`, #210); a recovery sweep's read, and `getQbitAddressUtxos`, still read and check every output. It rejects when the statistics fail or do not answer by the deadline, and with the caller's reason when the caller's signal aborts. The over-limit answers (`utxoLimitExceeded`) are unchanged.
- `getQbitAddressUtxosClassified`, `getQbitAddressUtxosClassifiedPastLimit`, `getQbitAddressUtxosClassifiedFromHistory`, `readQbitAddressUtxos` and `getQbitAddressUtxos` take `QbitClassifyOptions` (`signal`, `fundingTxReads`). A read of outputs for a recovery sweep, or through `getQbitAddressUtxos`, still needs every funding transaction and fails when one cannot be read; a read for a send or a plan reads only those of its young and unconfirmed outputs, and fails when one of those cannot be read (#210).

`getAddressSummary` is no longer a method the wallet's pages may call (`UI_CONTROLLER_METHODS`): nothing read its answer, and the balance refresh no longer asks for it.
