Add the account switcher's totals: `getQbitAccountTotals(addresses)` and `readQbitAccountTotal(address)`, which a wallet page may call (`UI_CONTROLLER_METHODS`). Each answers `QbitAccountTotal`: the address's confirmed and unconfirmed value together, from one small read of its statistics (`getAddressBalance`, `GET /address/:address`), however many outputs it holds.

`readQbitAccountTotal` answers from the kept total while it is fresh, `QBIT_ACCOUNT_TOTAL_FRESH_MS` (60 s) from when its answer arrived, and otherwise reads it on the network active when the call began, through that network's client, and answers with that `chainType` even if the network changes meanwhile. Each read is a fresh read, as `getTxOutspends(txid, { fresh: true })` is: a request of its own under a URL no other request has used (`QBIT_EXPLORER_FRESH_READ_PARAM`) and with `cache: 'no-store'`, never joined to an identical request already in flight nor answered from an HTTP cache, its answer still checked by the client library. A read of an address already in flight for the switcher is shared, unless it began before the latest submission through that client settled or while one was under way: a call then sends a request of its own. A call made after a submission settled is answered only with a total measured by a request sent after it, but for two cases: when a read begun after the submission failed, that call and those of the next `QBIT_ACCOUNT_TOTAL_RETRY_AFTER_MS` are answered with the last total measured, which may be from before the submission, with `error: true` and not fresh; and when the explorer's statistics still lag the submission, a fresh read answers the total from before it, fresh for only `QBIT_ACCOUNT_TOTAL_SETTLING_FRESH_MS` (below). Reads through one client keep at most `QBIT_ACCOUNT_TOTALS_MAX_READS_IN_FLIGHT` (4) in flight, so they never hold more than four of the six connections a browser opens to one host over HTTP/1.1, first come first served, on a `QbitReadLimiter` of their own, not the classification reads', each under one deadline for the call, its retries and backoff, `QBIT_ACCOUNT_TOTAL_READ_DEADLINE_MS` (8 s); the wait for a slot is not part of it. A failed read resolves with `error: true` and the last total measured, `null` if none was and never 0, and that address is not read again for `QBIT_ACCOUNT_TOTAL_RETRY_AFTER_MS` (10 s), unless the failed read began before the latest submission through that client settled or while one was under way: its failure says nothing of the explorer after the submission, and a call then reads. It rejects only for an address that is not a non-empty string. `getQbitAccountTotals` takes an array of at most `QBIT_ACCOUNT_TOTALS_MAX_ADDRESSES` (1,024) non-empty strings and refuses anything else (`null`, which a hole or `NaN` becomes over the port, included); it answers what is kept, one answer per entry in order, and reads nothing. Both answer with no total, and read nothing, for an address that is no account of a listed wallet, for an address that is no Qbit address of the active network (as a send checks its recipient: the Bitcoin-style `bc1…` and `tb1…` addresses a developer-mode read-only wallet, a watched address or a cold wallet shows) and for every address while the wallet reads locked, and both answer with no total, rather than reject, when the answer cannot be made. Which addresses are the wallet's own is worked out again only when the keyring service's list of wallets or the network changed, so a switcher reading a thousand accounts costs one derivation, not one per read; calls that come while the list is read share the read that follows it, never one begun before them, so a list the service must build again is built once for all of them.

Every successful `getAddressBalanceV2` keeps its `totalBalance`, dated when that read began, so the current account, which the home screen polls, rarely needs a read of its own. Its request is no fresh read, so nothing of it is kept when that read began before a submission through the client settled or within `QBIT_ACCOUNT_TOTAL_SETTLING_MS` after, or when it settles while one is under way: kept, it would outrank a switcher read begun before it, whose total from after the send would be dropped. The submission already left the kept total not fresh, and the switcher reads that account itself; `getAddressBalanceV2`'s own requests are unchanged. A read's total is kept only if, when it settles, no clear came since it began, the wallet does not read locked, and no total from a read that began later is kept already. Every submission of a transaction to an explorer (the send, the send plans, fee bumps, consolidation, a dapp's push and a recovery migration's) leaves the totals read through that client in place but no longer fresh, from when the bytes go out and again once it settles, whatever its outcome. A total the switcher read before it settled, or while it was under way, is kept but not fresh, and one read within `QBIT_ACCOUNT_TOTAL_SETTLING_MS` (30 s) after it is fresh for only `QBIT_ACCOUNT_TOTAL_SETTLING_FRESH_MS` (10 s), since the explorer's statistics may lag it; a broadcast refused before anything was sent changes nothing. The totals are in memory only, and forgotten wherever the outputs and funding transactions read are: when the wallet locks, when its data is reset, when a keyring is removed, when the network changes and when the regtest clients are dropped; what the own-address check keeps of the wallets is forgotten with them. A read waiting for a slot then sends nothing, one in flight is cancelled and gives up its slot at once, and their callers get no total; reads made after it wait behind none of them. After a wallet's removal, or after the regtest access is turned off while on another network, a `readQbitAccountTotal` call it overtook reads once more instead, if the wallet still reads unlocked on the network the call began on and the address is still an own account's, so a switcher left open shows the remaining wallets' totals rather than `--`.
