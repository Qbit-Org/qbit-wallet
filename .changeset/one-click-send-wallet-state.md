A payment that needs more of an account's outputs than one transaction can carry is sent as one plan the user confirms once. The send screen prices it with `planQbitSend` on the outputs and shape its preflight uses (`planQbitP2mrSendPreflight`) and shows what will happen instead of refusing: the transactions that combine the funds, then the payment, the total fee, exactly what the recipient receives, the signatures and about how long signing takes. MAX sends the most the account can send now: the plan of its confirmed outputs, which pays their whole balance less every fee and says that funds not confirmed yet are not included, only when it pays the recipient more than one transaction of the largest outputs can now (`qbitP2mrSendAllSendableSatoshis`); otherwise, a tie included, MAX sends that one transaction as before. When the balance covers the amount but not every fee, or the amount needs more transactions than one plan carries, it says so and offers the largest amount that can be sent as a choice, never in its place. While the account has a plan in progress (any phase but completed, as `useQbitSendPlan` reads it), the background starts no other: MAX then sends one transaction of the outputs that plan does not hold, with the note of what stays and a line that a payment in progress holds the rest, linked to it; an amount only a plan can pay keeps Next disabled with the payment in progress named and linked; a refusal offers only what one transaction sends. The send screen reads the outputs again when that plan starts, changes its transactions or ends, and reads the plan again when Next is answered `plan_open`. `useQbitSendPlanReviewScreenLogic` holds the one review and its one confirmation, with the restored-floor confirmation before the plan starts and a review that expires; `useQbitSendPlan` follows the selected account's plan on the active network (on mount, on `QBIT_SEND_PLAN_CHANGED`, on account and network changes, and every five seconds while it is open), drops a view older than one shown and never shows a plan for another account or network; `useQbitSendPlanScreenLogic` and `useQbitSendPlanEntryLogic` drive the progress screen, whose buttons are exactly the actions the plan offers and which shows the recipient, the amount and the total fee with its rate in every state of a readable plan, so the terms the user confirmed are in view before Continue, and the home screen's card. While a version of the payment that left the wallet without an answer may still confirm (`paymentMayConfirm`, read only as `true` or absent, and never of a plan paused `unreadable`), both say so whatever the plan paused for or is doing: the payment may still go through, the wallet keeps checking, the recipient is not to be paid another way until that is settled, what the user can do (only the actions the plan offers: raise its fee, send the same payment again) and that the funds it uses are in the wallet, held for it; no copy then says that nothing was sent, that the payment was not sent, or that it can be cancelled or reviewed again. `WalletController` gains `prepareQbitSendPlan`, `startQbitSendPlan`, `getQbitSendPlan`, `continueQbitSendPlan`, `cancelQbitSendPlan` and `dismissQbitSendPlan`, the routes `QbitSendPlanReviewScreen` and `QbitSendPlanScreen` are added, `TxSuccessScreen` takes the `sendPlanId` of a completed plan to remove once the user leaves, and `usePrepareSendQBTCallback` takes a fresh `utxoSnapshot`. A fee raise at a rate too low to replace what was sent names the lowest rate that works (#188).
