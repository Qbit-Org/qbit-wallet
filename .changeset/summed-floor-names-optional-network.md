A restore floor summed across Testnet4 and regtest that reaches the 2^28-signature warning threshold is still refused, and the refusal now says what to do. It shows both networks' counts, each with the read it came from (address statistics or transaction history), and its guidance depends on the restored network and on whose share is strictly the larger. On a Testnet4 restore it says to stop the local Regtest service, or turn off Local Regtest, then retry; when Testnet4's own share is the larger, it says that the Testnet4 count a retry would then restore is close to the threshold and can never be lowered. On a Regtest restore it points at the Testnet4 explorer, or at the local Regtest service when that share is the larger, and at the qbit Core recovery descriptors. The message is built from the background translation keys `qbit_restore_summed_floor_share_{testnet4,regtest}_{stats,history}` (the count) inside `qbit_restore_summed_floor_{testnet4,regtest}` or `qbit_restore_summed_floor_{testnet4,regtest}_own_share_larger` (the derivation index, the Testnet4 share, the regtest share and the threshold), with counts in the locale's digit groups. The refused keyring's scan stops at the refused account; the optional network stays in the scan while it answers, so keyrings scanned after it still count its signatures.
