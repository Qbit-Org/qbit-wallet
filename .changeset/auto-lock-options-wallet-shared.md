Change the auto-lock times. Breaking change: `getAutoLockTimes` lists six times, shortest first: 5 minutes (id 3), 15 minutes (8), 30 minutes (5), 1 hour (6), 3 hours (9) and 6 hours (10); ids 0, 1, 2, 4 and 7 (30 seconds, 1 minute, 3 minutes, 10 minutes and 4 hours) are retired and keep no meaning. The 6-hour label uses the new message key `n_hours_many`, the plural form Russian needs for 6. `DEFAULT_LOCKTIME_ID` is 5 (30 minutes) instead of 2 (3 minutes). `RETIRED_AUTO_LOCK_TIME_IDS` maps each retired id to the one that replaced it (0, 1 and 2 to 3, 4 to 8, 7 to 9), `SHORTEST_LOCKTIME_ID` is 3, and `isAutoLockTimeId(value)` tells whether a value is an id offered now. `resolveAutoLockTimeId(stored)` turns a stored `autoLockTimeId` into an id offered now: an offered id stays, a retired id becomes its replacement, `undefined` becomes the default, and anything else (an id this build does not know, a fractional or negative number, `null`, a string, NaN) becomes the shortest time. `repairStoredAutoLockTimeId(stored)` is what start-up stores again: a retired id becomes its replacement and a value that is no whole number of zero or more becomes the shortest time, while an offered id, `undefined` and a whole number that is neither offered nor retired, which a later build may have stored, stay as they are; it never changes the time `resolveAutoLockTimeId` reads. Breaking change: `getLockTimeInfo(id, t?)` takes any value and resolves it that way, so an unknown id gives the shortest time rather than the default.
