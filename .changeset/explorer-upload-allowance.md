A large transaction sent over a slow connection is no longer cut off while it uploads (#130). A broadcast's reply had 15 seconds from the start of the request, its upload included, so a consolidation or sweep of up to about 800 kB of hex needed about 430 kbit/s of upload; on a slower link it ended unknown, with its inputs reserved. A reply's budget now adds the time the request's body takes to upload at 16 KiB/s (`QBIT_EXPLORER_MIN_UPLOAD_BYTES_PER_SECOND`, about 130 kbit/s), up to the 5-minute attempt ceiling (`qbitExplorerReplyTimeoutMs`): about 64 seconds for the largest standard transaction, and about 15 seconds for a payment, as before. An upload that stalls still fails as a timeout once that budget is spent, with its outcome unknown and its inputs reserved. Each pass of a broadcast, a re-send's included, is sent only with that budget left (`qbitBroadcastMinPassMs`), and the broadcast's deadline is longer by the same upload time (`qbitBroadcastDeadlineMs`), so its fee bump check and its lookups keep their time. Speed up's 120-second assessment now starts once it has been through the send guard's queue, so a wait behind another send's broadcast no longer uses it up.
