'@qbit/wallet-background': minor

The send guard holds the outputs of an open send plan, its inputs and the outputs its merges will create: `holdOutpointsForPlan` takes a plan's hold all or nothing, refusing outputs a claim, an external signing lock, a recent broadcast, an open provider send request, another plan or a dapp signing recorded with its inputs (`recordProviderSigning` takes them now) already holds; `replacePlanOutpoints` swaps it in one step, `releasePlanOutpoints` lets go of all or part of it, and `restorePlanHolds` holds again what the stored plans held, reporting every overlap, a recent spend with the transaction recorded as spending it. Every entry point that admits a spend refuses one over a held output unless the caller presents the plan's `QbitSendPlanHold`, the object the guard minted for it, which `runWithNoOverlap`, `assertNoOverlap`, `runWithBroadcastClaim` and `broadcastIfAllowed` take as a new last argument; `getReservedOutpointKeys` lists held outputs, and `hasDependentSpend` counts them, so a plan's merge is never fee-bumped from outside the plan. `getPlanHeldOutpointKeys` lists what the plans hold on a network: a fee bump's assessment refuses a transaction one of whose inputs a plan holds (`held_by_send_plan`), as a completed plan holds its payment's until the payment confirms or the plan is dismissed, so Speed up is never offered for a payment its claim would be refused for, and is offered once the plan lets go. A plan is prepared only from outputs its own hold would take: `getPlanUnavailableOutpointKeys` lists the others, a dapp's signed inputs and open provider send requests included, which an ordinary send does not treat as reserved. The new `qbitSendPlanStore` keeps the open plans in one versioned document in extension storage, outside the vault and readable while the wallet is locked: every write is one storage write of the whole document, refused unless the caller presents the revision it read, one plan per network and account; `validateQbitSendPlanRecord` checks every record loaded or written, and an entry it cannot read, or a document of another version, is reported and kept on disk as it was (#188). Two entries of the same plan id, or for the same account on one network, are both unreadable until one is discarded; the other is then read as its plan at once, as a load of the document reads it, and the engine holds it again as a restart does, signing nothing of it before a continue.

The send plan engine (#188) carries a plan from its one confirmation to the recipient being paid, without the popup staying open: `prepareQbitSendPlan` prices the whole plan for the selected account (every merge, the payment, the exact amount, one signature budget from a batch of the plan's transactions, and a signing time) and keeps it as a draft that reserves nothing; `startQbitSendPlan` holds the plan's outputs and stores it in one coupled step, then signs each merge and broadcasts it at once, signs the payment last and stores it, waits for every merge to confirm, and broadcasts the payment, also while the wallet is locked; `getQbitSendPlan`, `continueQbitSendPlan`, `cancelQbitSendPlan` and `dismissQbitSendPlan` show and steer it, and every change reaches the wallet's pages as `QBIT_SEND_PLAN_CHANGED` with the plan's id and a revision that only grows, restarts included. Every broadcast is written ahead as `unknown` and goes back only when the broadcast path proves it was not sent; a step whose outcome is unknown is looked up, never sent blindly again. A lock pauses the signing, which goes on by itself after an unlock in the same session, and drops the drafts, whichever way the wallet locks (the keyring's own lock after an address-type change it cannot roll back included, which reaches the plans from the keyring's `lock` event); a restart needs the user's continue before anything more is signed; a timer, every unlock and every page that reads the plan advance it. The auto-lock is held while a plan signs, for at most a ceiling derived from the plan's signing time, and closing the popup does not stop it. A restored-floor key's risk is confirmed once, for the plan's first signature, before start. A replan keeps a sent merge that has not confirmed as a carried step whose inputs stay held (`QbitSendPlanRecord.carried`, `paidFee`), and a completed plan's record drops the payment's signed bytes. A dapp's signing is now recorded with its inputs in the same step as its overlap check (`QBIT_PROVIDER_SIGNING`), so a plan never holds outputs a dapp holds signed bytes for, and a spend refused over a plan's outputs carries the code `QBIT_SEND_PLAN_HELD` across the UI port. The plan store is exported from the package root.

A plan's fee can be raised from inside it, reviewed and confirmed once like a replan (#188). `raise_merge_fee`, offered once a merge has stayed unconfirmed for long (`slowMerge`, set only with it) while the plan waits for its merges or is paused for anything but a lock, never while it signs (each merge it sends writes the plan, so a raise reviewed then could not start), prices every step that has not confirmed again at a higher rate, the payment included: each merge in the network is replaced by one spending the same inputs, which must replace it by the fee bump's rule (now `qbitReplacementFeeAllowed`, which `assessQbitFeeBump` uses too), and the payment is built again on the replacements. `raise_payment_fee`, offered when the network refused the payment for its fee, or refused it without a reason, builds the payment again over the same inputs. For an amount the amount never changes and the extra fees come out of the change; for everything the amount is recomputed and the review states the one it replaces. A rate that cannot replace what is in the network, or is not above the plan's, is refused as `fee_rate_too_low` with the lowest rate that works. A raised merge keeps the version it replaces (`QbitSendPlanStepRecord.replaces`) until one of the two confirms, and both versions' outputs stay held until then; if the replaced version confirms instead, the replacement and the payment signed on it are void and the plan pauses for a replan. The send guard admits a plan's replacement of its own merge only under that plan's hold (`QbitSendPlanReplacement`, a new last argument of `broadcastIfAllowed`, `runWithNoOverlap` and `assertNoOverlap`), checked as a fee bump's claim is.

The engine also reads each plan transaction from the explorer at most once a tick, whatever advances the plan, except that every merge the payment spends is read afresh immediately before each dispatch of the payment, which goes only if all of them are confirmed then (a merge found unconfirmed sends the plan back to waiting); writes a plan back as it was when a revision's start is undone for an unconfirmed restored-floor challenge, and starts an undone new plan's draft again under a new id, an undo writing back or removing only the very record its start wrote (a cancel another page made meanwhile stands, and the start answers `draft_expired`); revises no plan whose payment may be in the network; looks sent merges up before planning a revision; leaves a plan being started to its start when a page reads it in between; and decides a refusal that carries only an RPC code, as the production explorer relays one, from what the explorer can still tell.

Every transaction of a plan records the rate it was priced at (`QbitSendPlanStepRecord.feeRate`), and none pays a fee the wallet's own pricing would not give it (#188). Before each signature the engine checks, from the PSBT itself, that its size and fee are exactly its price at that rate (`qbitSendPlanFeeProblem`: a payment of an amount without change may also hold a remainder too small to return as change, as the planner folds one, within the maximum total fee), the rate within policy and never above the plan's; `validateQbitSendPlanRecord` refuses on load and on every write a step whose size or fee is not that price. A stored plan that fails either check is no plan the wallet made: refused on load it is unreadable, and failing the check before a signature it pauses `unreadable`, offering only cancel, with nothing signed. A record changed on disk can still move the plan's rate within policy, which the plan's view states with its total fee.

What a stored plan shows is what it sends (#188). `validateQbitSendPlanRecord` also refuses, on load and on every write, a record whose transactions are not what it states: an account address that is not its key's on the plan's network; a step whose unsigned PSBT is not that step's transaction by the check made before each signature (its id, its inputs, a merge's one output to the account, its fee at its rate), the payment's paying exactly the recipient's script on the plan's network the amount and the change to the account, and nothing else of value; or a signed payment that is not that same transaction byte for byte, every input finalized. Such a record is unreadable from the moment it is loaded, holds nothing and offers only its cancel. Immediately before it broadcasts a stored payment the engine checks it again the same way, and pauses the plan `unreadable` instead of sending one that fails.

The recipient is paid once whatever version of the payment confirms (#188). A plan records what became of its payment's bytes once they left the wallet (`QbitSendPlanStepRecord.dispatch`: `refused` when every broadcast was refused, `unknown` when one ended without a verdict), and a raise of the payment's fee keeps every earlier version whose bytes left the wallet (`QbitSendPlanRecord.earlierPayments`, at most 16), each over exactly the payment's inputs in the same order: a raise of the payment's fee builds the new version over the inputs of the one it replaces, in their order, after a raise of a merge's fee too. A version found in the network, or found spending the payment's inputs, is the payment: the plan completes with it, its id, amount and fee. A transaction that is no version but spends the payment's inputs and pays the recipient the amount completes the plan too (`QbitSendPlanRecord.paidBy`); what spends a payment input is identified this way before the plan pauses `outputs_spent` and before a revision is prepared. While a version whose broadcast had no verdict may still confirm, neither a replan nor a cancel is offered, and `cancelQbitSendPlan` leaves the plan as it is, holds included: proven absent, such a payment is sent again over the same inputs (Continue, offered whatever the plan paused for), or its fee raised over them, which cannot pay twice, and the plan completes once any version is found. Once an input of it is found spent by a transaction that is no version of it, or a merge it spends is shown spent elsewhere, no version can confirm: it is forgotten, and a replan and a cancel may be offered. The engine's timer keeps advancing a plan paused while such a version may confirm, as it does one paused while its payment may be in the network, and stops once nothing is in doubt: with every page closed, the plan completes once a version is found, and, reading what spends the payment's inputs a few transactions at a time, forgets the versions and pauses `outputs_spent` once an input is spent by a transaction that is no version of it. The plan's view counts such a version as sent (`sent`) and says that it may still confirm (`paymentMayConfirm`) until the plan completes; its pause's technical detail is the pause's own. Once the payment's bytes have left the wallet a merge's fee is no longer raised, so every version keeps the same inputs.

A plan's review never outlives what it depends on (#188): one that carries a restored-floor challenge, a new plan's, a raise's or a replan's, expires when that challenge does (the keyring's `QBIT_RESTORED_FLOOR_CHALLENGE_TTL_MS`, counted from when prepare asked for it), or after the draft's own ten minutes, whichever is first, and its draft is dropped then with its batch and challenge; a start after that answers `draft_expired`.

A replan's `paidFee` counts only the fees of merges an earlier version of the plan sent that are, or may still be, in the network or confirmed (#188): a carried merge proven absent takes its fee off, so the plan's total fee and its `sent` no longer count a fee that was never paid.

Removing a wallet ends the send plans that belong to it, on every Qbit network, as a cancel ends them (#188): the plans of the accounts it signs for that no wallet staying signs for. A watch-only, readonly or cold wallet holds no plan, so removing one that lists the address of an account another wallet signs for ends nothing. For the plans it ends: their drafts go, signing stops, nothing more is signed or broadcast for them, the stored signed payment goes with the record, and holds are let go of under the cancel rules (a merge whose outcome is unknown keeps its inputs held until it is found or proven absent, the plan cancelling meanwhile). A plan whose payment may already be in the network, a version of whose payment may still confirm, or that completed, cannot be un-sent: its record and its holds go all the same, and the removal logs that one may have paid. An unreadable stored entry filed under one of the removed accounts is dropped too. Work under way for those accounts loses to the removal, which waits for it before it ends their plans, and none begins meanwhile: a start answers `draft_expired` and nothing it wrote or held stays, a continue is refused and authorises nothing, a prepare keeps no draft, and a broadcast written ahead does not leave. Before, the plan went on with no screen able to show or cancel it, and its signed payment could still be broadcast once its merges confirmed. Where a removal cannot derive the accounts of the removed wallet, or of a wallet that stays and lists one of its keys, on the other derivation path (the failure is logged), it ends no plan from a guess: while a stored plan, an entry the store cannot read, a draft, a start or a continue is on that path's networks, under any account, the removal is refused before anything is removed, nothing removed and nothing ended, for the user to try again; otherwise it goes on, and no plan work begins on those networks until it is over (`holdNetworksIfIdle`).

Resetting the wallet stops its send plans before anything else is reset, and keeps them stopped until the reset is over (#188): nothing of a plan is signed, broadcast, written or held meanwhile, a start, continue or advance under way ends first, and no plan or hold is left once the reset is done. A reset that fails leaves the plans as a restart does: a plan is held again from its record, and what its own transactions' broadcasts recorded as spent (the inputs of a merge that confirmed) does not pause it `outputs_spent`, while a spend recorded for any other transaction still does.

A plan paused `unreadable` because its transactions failed the engine's checks shows nothing of what it states (#188): its view is the stand-in of a plan the store cannot read (no recipient, amount, rate, fee or steps), cancel its one action, and it keeps that view, offering nothing more, while its cancel waits for a merge whose outcome is unknown. It stays so until its cancel ends it: nothing that pauses, settles or restores a plan changes that pause (a merge a raise replaced found confirmed instead, another stored plan found holding its outputs at start-up, its payment found in the network), so it is never shown with its figures, offered a replan or completed from its record, and nothing of it is signed or sent; its cancel is offered whatever its payment's state, since nothing else ends it.

What a continue approves is recorded (#188): the signature figures of every transaction of the plan that may still need a signature (each merge that has not confirmed, and the payment while it is unsigned) are read before the plan does anything else, whatever the plan resumes into, after the unlock when a lock came first. Continue is offered and accepted only while the wallet is unlocked, whatever the plan paused for: the UI port carries no proof of the password, so a continue while it is locked, from a page left open or any other caller, is refused and changes nothing, and an unlock never signs what no continue made while unlocked approved. Every later signing in the session must continue them exactly, as after a start, so a merge signed again after the user continued into waiting or sending is checked as one signed after a start, and a signature another request made with the key since, after a lock or not, waits for the next continue. The engine never opens a signature batch on figures the user did not approve: a signature that no recorded approval covers pauses the plan `interrupted`.
