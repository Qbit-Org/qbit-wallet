Make the explorer check before a Speed up broadcast harder to fool. The check now reads what spends the original twice, 5 seconds apart (`QBIT_FEE_BUMP_RECHECK_INTERVAL_MS`), and the bump goes out only when both reads show nothing spending it, so a payment the explorer shows only on the second read is no longer evicted. Both reads bypass the browser's HTTP cache, so the second is never the first answer replayed. The whole check, both reads and the pause included, still has 15 seconds (`QBIT_FEE_BUMP_RECHECK_WAIT_MS`) within the broadcast's deadline. Every read is aborted when the check gives up, and a first read too slow to leave time for the second refuses the bump at once. A send claim restored after a background restart is no longer trusted for what it says it replaces, since the guard's records that could confirm it went with the restart: a claim saying it replaces nothing could be a fee bump that evicts its original's payments. Its broadcast now also reads, twice and fresh, what spends each of its inputs. It goes out only when nothing but its own transaction, or the original it names, spends them; otherwise it is refused before dispatch and its claim stays held for a retry. `QbitWalletEsploraClient` and `QbitWalletMempoolClient` take `fresh` in `getTxOutspends` options (`QbitTxOutspendsOptions`) for such a read. `QbitSendGuardService.broadcastIfAllowed` hands its broadcast callback `restored` for a restored claim. `assertNoExplorerSpendConflict` replaces `assertNoExplorerDependentSpend`, and checks the inputs of a restored claim as well as a fee bump's original.
