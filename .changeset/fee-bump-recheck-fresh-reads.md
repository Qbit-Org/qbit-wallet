'@qbit/wallet-background': patch
---

Make the explorer check before a Speed up broadcast harder to fool. The check now reads what spends the original twice, 5 seconds apart (`QBIT_FEE_BUMP_RECHECK_INTERVAL_MS`), and the bump goes out only when both reads show nothing spending it, so a payment the explorer shows only on the second read is no longer evicted, and one it shows only on the first is not either. Every read goes out under a URL of its own, with a random `qbit_fresh` query parameter (`QBIT_EXPLORER_FRESH_READ_PARAM`) and `cache: 'no-store'`, so neither the browser's cache nor one in front of the explorer can answer it with an earlier reply. The whole check, both reads and the pause included, still has at most 15 seconds (`QBIT_FEE_BUMP_RECHECK_WAIT_MS`), and now leaves the broadcast's submission its reply budget (`QBIT_BROADCAST_MIN_PASS_MS`) after it: with less of the broadcast's deadline left than two reads of at least 2 seconds each (`QBIT_FEE_BUMP_RECHECK_MIN_READ_MS`) and the pause need, it refuses before reading anything. A first read that leaves the second less than that refuses at once instead of pausing. Every read is aborted when the check gives up. A send claim restored after a background restart is no longer trusted for what it says it replaces, since the guard's records that could confirm it went with the restart: a claim saying it replaces nothing could be a fee bump that evicts its original's payments. Its broadcast now also reads, twice and fresh, what spends each of its inputs, one read per transaction they are outputs of and at most four at a time (`QBIT_FEE_BUMP_RECHECK_MAX_PARALLEL_READS`). It goes out only when nothing but its own transaction, or the original it names, spends them. Otherwise it is refused before dispatch ("Qbit transaction spends an input another transaction already spends") and its claim stays held for a retry. `QbitWalletEsploraClient` and `QbitWalletMempoolClient` take `fresh` in `getTxOutspends` options (`QbitTxOutspendsOptions`) for such a read, which never joins an ordinary read of the same route. `QbitSendGuardService.broadcastIfAllowed` hands its broadcast callback `restored` for a restored claim. `assertNoExplorerSpendConflict` replaces `assertNoExplorerDependentSpend`, and checks the inputs of a restored claim as well as a fee bump's original. `qbitOutspendSpender` and `qbitTxSpendersFromOutspends` validate an outspends answer for both.
