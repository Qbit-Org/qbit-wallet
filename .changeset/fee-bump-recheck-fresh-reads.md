'@qbit/wallet-background': patch
---

Make the explorer check before a Speed up broadcast harder to fool. The check now reads what spends the original twice, 5 seconds apart (`QBIT_FEE_BUMP_RECHECK_INTERVAL_MS`), and the bump goes out only when both reads show nothing spending it, so a payment the explorer shows only on the second read is no longer evicted, and one it shows only on the first is not either. Every read goes out under a URL of its own, with a random `qbit_fresh` query parameter (`QBIT_EXPLORER_FRESH_READ_PARAM`) and `cache: 'no-store'`, so neither the browser's cache nor one in front of the explorer can answer it with an earlier reply. Every read is aborted when the check gives up, and the first failed read or malformed answer makes it give up at once.

A send claim restored after a background restart is no longer trusted for what it says it replaces, since the guard's records that could confirm it went with the restart: a claim saying it replaces nothing could be a fee bump that evicts its original's payments. Its broadcast now also reads, twice and fresh, what spends each of its inputs, once per transaction they are outputs of. It goes out only when nothing but its own transaction, or the original it names, spends them. Otherwise it is refused before dispatch ("Qbit transaction spends an input another transaction already spends"), and its claim stays held for a retry. A restored send that is not a fee bump and whose inputs cannot be checked is refused as "Could not check that the inputs of this transaction are still unspent".

The check reads at most eight transactions at a time (`QBIT_OUTSPENDS_CONCURRENCY`), through `findQbitOutpointSpenders`, which `findQbitSpentOutpoints` now uses as well. Its budget is the pause plus 5 seconds a round for each eight reads (`QBIT_FEE_BUMP_RECHECK_WAVE_MS`), 15 seconds (`QBIT_FEE_BUMP_RECHECK_WAIT_MS`) for up to eight reads at the default pause, so a restored consolidation of many inputs from many transactions can pass. The budget never exceeds 65 seconds (`QBIT_FEE_BUMP_RECHECK_MAX_WAIT_MS`), which covers the largest standard transaction's 106 inputs. It also never takes the 15 seconds the broadcast's submission needs (`QBIT_BROADCAST_MIN_PASS_MS`). If what is left cannot give each group of eight reads 2 seconds (`QBIT_FEE_BUMP_RECHECK_MIN_READ_MS`) in both rounds, plus the pause, the check refuses before reading anything. A first round that leaves the second less refuses at once instead of pausing. Those refusals, and reads that time out, are reported as `timeout` rather than `guard_blocked`, and as `unknown` after an earlier broadcast of the same bytes ended unknown. A re-send that the check delays past the re-send window is not sent.

`QbitWalletEsploraClient` and `QbitWalletMempoolClient` take `fresh` in `getTxOutspends` options (`QbitTxOutspendsOptions`) for such a read, which never joins an ordinary read of the same route. `QbitSendGuardService.broadcastIfAllowed` hands its broadcast callback what it must check on the explorer, a `QbitExplorerSpendCheck` built by `qbitExplorerSpendCheckFor`, on every call, `{}` when there is nothing to check. A broadcast handed no check is refused before dispatch (`assertQbitExplorerSpendCheck`), never sent unchecked. `assertNoExplorerSpendConflict` replaces `assertNoExplorerDependentSpend`, and throws `QbitExplorerSpendCheckTimeoutError` when it refuses for lack of time. `qbitOutspendSpender` and `qbitTxSpendersFromOutspends` validate an outspends answer for every reader, and `findQbitOutpointSpenders` hands its first failure to `onFailure` so the caller can abort the reads still in flight.
