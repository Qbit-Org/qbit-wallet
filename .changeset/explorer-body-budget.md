Let explorer reads with large bodies finish on slow links (#56). `@qbit-oss/api` times each attempt from the request to the last byte of the body (15 s by default), so a history page of consolidation transactions, several MB to tens of MB of witness hex, failed all three attempts on a link that could not deliver it in 15 s, and the account's history, the PQC signature counter observation and the over-limit output read failed with it. The wallet's explorer clients (`QbitWalletEsploraClient`, `QbitWalletMempoolClient`) now time their requests through the new `createQbitExplorerFetch`. An address's history pages and transaction reads must start answering within `QBIT_EXPLORER_HEADERS_TIMEOUT_MS` (15 s); a body that then sends no bytes for `QBIT_EXPLORER_BODY_IDLE_TIMEOUT_MS` (15 s) is aborted and released, and `QBIT_EXPLORER_ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT_MS` (300 s) caps one attempt. Every other reply, a broadcast's included, keeps the 15 s budget for headers and body together (`QBIT_EXPLORER_REPLY_TIMEOUT_MS`). A reply body longer than `QBIT_EXPLORER_MAX_BODY_BYTES` (64 MiB) fails as unreadable. A stalled connection still fails as a `timeout` and is retried as before, and a submission's reply keeps its outcome classification.
